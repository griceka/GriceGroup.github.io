Research

The Grice Lab uses molecular inorganic chemistry to investigate catalysis, small-molecule activation, and chemically important transformations.

Small-molecule activation

Strong-bond transformations

Understanding and controlling reactions involving strong bonds, including alkane dehydrogenation and related transformations.

Catalysis

CO₂ reduction

Studying molecular catalysts and mechanisms for converting carbon dioxide into useful reduced products, including fuels.

Bioinorganic chemistry

Metal complexes in biology

Investigating metal-containing systems and coordination chemistry underlying biological function and inhibition.

Sustainable chemistry

Green chemistry

Developing and teaching more sustainable approaches to chemical synthesis and laboratory education.

Research students

DePaul undergraduate and M.S. students interested in inorganic chemistry, catalysis, green chemistry, bioinorganic chemistry, or computational chemistry are encouraged to get in touch.

Students do not need to be chemistry or biochemistry majors; curiosity, some chemistry preparation, and a willingness to learn are the important starting points.
