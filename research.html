The Grice Lab

Research

Our work uses inorganic and organometallic chemistry to understand molecular structure, reactivity, catalysis, and biologically relevant metal chemistry.

01 · Strong bonds

Activation and transformation of strong chemical bonds

Research interests include alkane dehydrogenation, C–H activation, and related transformations that challenge conventional approaches to chemical reactivity.

02 · CO₂

Carbon dioxide reduction

Molecular transition-metal complexes are investigated as catalysts for electrochemical and chemical reduction of CO₂, including pathways toward fuels and other useful products.

03 · Bioinorganic

Metals in biological chemistry

Studies include zinc and gold complexes, enzyme inhibitors, biomimetic complexes, and computational approaches to understanding metal–ligand interactions.

04 · Computation

Computational inorganic chemistry

DFT calculations and molecular modeling are used alongside synthesis, spectroscopy, crystallography, and reactivity studies to develop mechanistic insight.

05 · Education

Green and sustainable chemistry education

Research and scholarship also address safer solvents, sustainable laboratory practices, course-based research, and open resources for chemistry educators.

Research at DePaul

Much of this work is carried out with undergraduate and M.S. students, often in collaboration with researchers at other institutions and national laboratories. The group has used techniques including NMR, electrochemistry, X-ray crystallography, X-ray absorption spectroscopy, and computational chemistry.
